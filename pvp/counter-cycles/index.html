Counter Cycles

Rock, paper, scissors hides inside RTS openings, fighting game mix-ups and MOBA team comps. Each gets a wheel; the RTS one also plays out on a map, and the fighting game one with Street Fighter sprites, for anyone who has never played either.

Arrows point from the winner to what it beats. Hover anything; click a node to pin it.

The slide’s text notation, “A < B”, reads “A loses to B”. Every wheel shows that notation underneath, and hovering a “<” lights up its arrow.
RTS openings

Rush · Turtle · Expand

Each opening is a bet on what the opponent is doing. Green arrows: what it beats. Red: what beats it.

Turtle beats Rush, Expand beats Turtle, Rush beats Expand. “Watch the meta rotate” walks the cycle: when everyone does one thing, its counter becomes popular, which creates the opening for the next counter.
RTS openings, for people who never played one

Three openings, one clock

Three players, same start: a base and 12 workers mining blue crystals, which is all the money in the game. Everything else is how they spend it. Rush stops making workers at 14 and pours the rest into two Gateways and early zealots that walk across the map at about 2:30. Turtle builds a wall of buildings across its ramp and Photon Cannons behind it, and its army never leaves home; one base caps it at about 22 workers. Expand spends 400 on a second base before it has a single soldier, then a third, and only after 4:00 turns the bigger income into an army four soldiers at a time. Watch the money bar on the right of each row. Timings and costs are illustrative, loosely Protoss.

Each counter, played out

Each button is one arrow of the wheel on the same map. Turtle beats Rush: the zealots arrive at a closed wall with cannons behind it and die without killing anything, and the rusher has 14 workers to the turtle's 22. Expand beats Turtle: nothing punishes the early second base, so by 6:00 Expand has three bases, twice the workers and four Gateways, and walks through four cannons. Rush beats Expand: the zealots arrive while the new base has workers and no defenders, kill nine workers and then the base, before it has paid back its 400. The chart under the map is soldiers (solid) and workers (dashed) over time. Click the map to pause, or drag the clock.
fighting game mix-ups

Attack · Block · Grab

The same triangle at a scale of fractions of a second. Green: beats. Red: loses to.

Block beats Attack, Grab beats Block, Attack beats Grab. The RTS cycle plays out over minutes and whole matches; this one plays out every few seconds, which is why fighting games feel like reading minds.
fighting game mix-ups: the wheel, played out

Each mix-up, played out

Each button is one arrow of the wheel, played by Ryu and Chun-Li in Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike at a quarter of the game’s speed. The sprites, boxes and frame data are the game’s, from the Baston hitbox viewer; the reactions (block spark, hit freeze, the toss) are drawn on top. Block beats Attack: Ryu’s sweep is −13 on block, so after Chun-Li blocks it she is free 13 frames before he is, long enough for her Crouching Forward to hit his leg. Grab beats Block: Chun-Li holds guard waiting for a strike, Ryu dashes in and throws her, and guard does nothing against a throw. Attack beats Grab: a throw reaches only 49 pixels, so Ryu has to dash in, and Chun-Li’s kick, pressed as he starts moving, hits him mid-dash before he is in range. The frame strip under the stage colours every frame of both players: startup, active, recovery, guard, stun. Click the stage to pause, or drag the slider.
MOBA roles

A Five-Role Cycle

The slide’s chain, Marksman < Mage < Assassin < Tank < Bruiser < Marksman, is the outer ring. The 2-stage model adds the inner star: Assassin beats Mage and Marksman, Tank beats Assassin and Mage, Bruiser beats Tank and Assassin, Marksman beats Bruiser and Tank, Mage beats Marksman and Bruiser.
why two stages?

No Even Matchups

With five options and one stage, half the pairs have no arrow between them, so a pick only matters against two of the four other roles. Two stages connect every pair: each role has two winning matchups and two losing ones, and the cycle stays balanced.
