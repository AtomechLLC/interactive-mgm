Where each gate comes from

“Don’t move on to the next step until the previous has been satisfied.” Each gate is one lesson in Alex Brazie’s series, written in 2012 after Burning Crusade.

Gate 1 · Lesson 1ClarityCan the player tell what it is and what it will do?The tool is communication: unique art, sound and a telegraph, like StarCraft II’s nuclear launch or Super Mario World’s timer warning.Lesson 1. Clarity → Gate 2 · Lesson 2CareDoes the player have a reason to act on it?Tuning and emotion set how much players care. The Super Mushroom was unclear, yet players chased it because it saved a life.Lesson 2. Care → Gate 3 · Lesson 3ResponseIs there something the player can do about it?Three kinds: react as it happens, prepare before it happens, recuperate after it happened.Lesson 3. Response → Gate 4 · Lesson 4SatisfactionDoes doing it feel rewarding?Reward the intended action, pace peaks and valleys, and give a win its ceremony.Lesson 4: Satisfaction → Gate 5 · Lesson 5FitDoes it belong in this game, its theme and its audience?Fit the theme, fit the audience, and keep the game’s own identity: no arm wrestling in chess.Fit →
