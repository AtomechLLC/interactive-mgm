# Audit Gallery: thirteen games through the nine fundamentals

Each game run through the nine fundamentals of action games (Counter Circle × timing, deck slides 242-255), the same grid as Audit Any Moveset. A gap is a cell the kit could fill but does not; n/a is a cell the game has no way to express. Move tags and risk are this page’s reading. Interactive: `composition/audits/index.html`. ## Subnautica

Subnautica fills the defensive row and leaves Punish and Counterplay empty. Most moves are evasive, and the one offensive tool does almost no damage. The gaps are the design: the player is never paid for reading a leviathan, only for not being there.

## God of War

Both eras fill all nine cells, so the audit shows no gaps; the change is which moves fill them. The Norse games hand Initiate to a companion, make Zone a throw-and-recall gamble, and move Setup from a once-a-fight rage meter to a weapon charge used every exchange.

## Cyberpunk 2077

The game fills all nine cells, but no single build does. The blades build keeps Initiate, Punish and the defensive row, mostly as high-risk moves, with Zone, Counterplay and Response empty. The netrunner keeps eight cells, almost all low-risk, and lacks Response. Hybrid builds are how a player fills the grid.

## Portal 2

Chell’s kit fills seven cells with no weapon. Punish and React are gaps: turrets never leave an opening worth punishing, and health refills by itself, so nothing answers a hit. Portal 2 reads the grid as puzzle language: the "opponent" is the room, and Setup carries the most moves.

## Clair Obscur: Expedition 33

A turn-based game that fills eight cells, because parry, dodge and jump turn the enemy’s turn into real-time defense. Zone is the one gap: there is no space on the battlefield to control. The turn structure moves most offense into Setup and Punish.

## StarCraft: Brood War

The Terran army fills all nine cells, but no single unit does: Initiate belongs to the Marine, Zone to the Siege Tank, Punish to the Science Vessel. A real-time strategy moveset is an army’s, spread across units. The economy (gathering, expanding, production) is off the grid, and it decides most games.

Off the grid: Gather, Build workers, Expand, Supply, Production.

## Mario Kart 8 Deluxe

The item set fills all nine cells, but the player does not choose the cell: the item roulette deals items by race position. The leader is dealt Bananas, Green Shells and Coins (Mitigate, Zone, React); the back of the pack is dealt Bullet Bills, Stars and Lightning (Initiate, Setup, Punish). The player’s choice is timing. The Spiny Shell is the stretch case: it sits in Punish but punishes leading, not a mistake.

## Minecraft

The grid fills, but only by stretching. Counterplay exists only against players: mobs never block. Zone and Reflex answer the world, not an opponent (darkness, falls). And most of play sits off the grid: mining, crafting and building have no "them" to answer. The framework audits Minecraft’s combat, not Minecraft.

Off the grid: Mine, Craft, Build, Farm, Trade, Explore.

## Celeste

Madeline’s kit is all movement, so the offensive row nearly empties: Zone and Punish are not gaps but impossible, because she has no hitbox. Read with the mountain as the opponent, the defensive and predictive rows fill, and instant respawn turns every Response into a retry.

## Deadlock

The shared kit fills seven cells; React and Zone are its gaps, left for heroes and items to fill. Abrams (picked as the clearest brawler) adds two React moves and stacks Initiate and Punish, but leaves Zone empty, which fits a hero built to close distance rather than hold space.

## Factorio

The grid only sees the defense layer, and the factory automates most of it: turrets do the zoning, the reflexes and the punishing. Counterplay is a gap, because biters have no pattern to read beyond following pollution. The real moveset is the factory, and its verbs (belts, inserters, trains) sit off the grid.

Off the grid: Mine, Craft, Place, Belts, Inserters, Trains, Research.

## Slay the Spire

Eight cells fill; Reflex is impossible because the enemy turn takes no input. Intents turn every telegraph into exact information, so Mitigate becomes arithmetic (Block equal to the number shown) and Counterplay becomes deck rules learned per enemy.

## Legends of Runeterra

Two champions, both empty in the defensive row. In a card game the champion is half the moveset: the second region’s spells (Barrier, Deny, healing) fill the other row. Draven covers three cells, all bets on striking first; Teemo covers five, nearly all low-risk and delayed, and wins through the opponent’s own draws.
